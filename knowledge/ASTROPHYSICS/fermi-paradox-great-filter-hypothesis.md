The Fermi Paradox and Great Filter Hypothesis: Where Is Everybody?

Overview

The Fermi Paradox highlights a simple but unsettling contradiction:

If the universe is so large and statistically rich in habitable worlds, why do we see no evidence of extraterrestrial civilizations?

The Great Filter hypothesis proposes a possible answer: somewhere along the path from dead matter to interstellar civilization, there is a highly unlikely or destructive step that prevents most life from advancing.


---

Origins

Fermi Paradox

Named after: Enrico Fermi

Year: 1950

Core question: “Where is everybody?”


Despite billions of stars and potentially habitable planets, no clear evidence of intelligent life has been detected.


---

Great Filter

Proposed by: Robin Hanson

Year: 1998

Core idea: At least one evolutionary step is extremely improbable or self-destructive

Possible Great Filters

Behind Us (We may be rare)

Abiogenesis

Life emerging from non-life may be extremely rare.

Complex Cell Evolution

Transition from simple to complex cells may require improbable events.

Multicellularity

Complex organisms may be an evolutionary bottleneck.

Intelligence

Technological intelligence may be extremely uncommon.


---

Artificial Intelligence

Resource Limits

Technological growth may outpace planetary sustainability.

Self-replicating or hostile machines could prevent expansion.

Core Insights

The Fermi Paradox questions why the universe appears silent despite high probability of life

The Great Filter suggests one or more extreme barriers prevent technological civilizations from becoming common

The critical uncertainty is whether the filter is behind us or ahead of us

If ahead, humanity may face existential risks tied to its own technological development

If behind, intelligent life may be extraordinarily rare

The paradox informs both scientific exploration and long-term survival strategy
